Vibe coding is a phrase that gets people generally upset. Coding is a creative act, like writing, making music or drawing. ~About a year ago, [I talked about my experiences with AI coding](/posts/764) and it was very mixed. Like many people in the software engineering industry, this summer, that changed drastically. I went from AI helping me do my job to it doing seventy percent or more of my coding, leaving me to do other things. I have mixed feelings about this because the ethics of generative LLMs are not great: from their slurping of archives they don't share to the environmental impacts of super large data centers.

But as the tools have gotten better, I am using them more at work, and also in my personal life. I still love coding for fun, but AI has made it so that I can execute on some side projects that I either did not really want to learn (mobile development) or fix issues that I don't have time for. I am going to talk through some projects I have built which are bringing me joy because they now exist and I never had the energy, time or knowledge to make exist in the world.

## Etu

I have written about this app [before](/posts/765). It is my ongoing journaling app. I had Claude build [a mobile app](https://github.com/icco/etu-mobile), which I guess I didn't really need because the mobile web app worked great. But now having offline support on my phone has been great.

## Playgrounditor

[![Playgrounditor on a tablet in a coffeeshop.](https://images.natwelch.com/photos/2026/0S7HJWJDMHM57.jpg?w=1024)](https://images.natwelch.com/photos/2026/0S7HJWJDMHM57.jpg)

This is something I have been searching for... a very long time. I just want a simple text editor for editing files that has offline caching. I finally gave up my search the other week when I was sitting in a park next to a playground wanting to edit a blog post. Github's web editor is ok, but not great, plus does not handle offline well at all. So I sent opencode off to go build the app in react-native.

So far it's been a great success! I am even writing this post using this app as we speak.

## Glanced

This is a previously abandoned idea that I wanted to revive. I tried my hand at making an android widget for tracking tasks. It did not work well, or at all. So instead I had opencode reboot it as a version of my favorite [Ugmonk's Analog Cards](https://ugmonk.com/en-gb/collections/analog). I find the idea wonderful, but the cards are too expensive, and I generally don't want to carry them around or travel with them. 

So my idea here is that I'll have a mobile version that mimics the real physical product. We'll see if it works.

## Goals

A previous project I coded by hand, but had abandoned, which was a goal tracker that texted you reminders. It did not work well as it had very little state tracking. I had opencode reimagine the idea. It's not working yet, but loving the upgrades and ideas that are changing with it.

## Art

Art was originally a two piece AI assistant:

1. Read my email, and archive anything I don't care about.
2. Add time blocks to force me to stop working and take a mental break.

The second feature did not work well for me at all. I need to manually control my calendar, it's too important and complicated for an algorithm to be able to handle intelligently or in any way that is useful.

The first feature though, has been amazing. I had to add tweak the prompt and rules a few times, but it is now at the point I never see email I don't want to see, and don't miss things I do. It also is incredibly token efficient now, costing me about $0.30 in tokens each month.
